571 CE · 53 BH
The birth of the Prophet Muhammad, son of Abdullah, peace be upon him.
The house of Abu Talib in the Shi'b of Banu Hashim
The biographers and historians have differed in determining the day, month, and year of his birth, peace be upon him, but they agreed that his birth, peace be upon him, was on a Monday in the Year of the Elephant. Ibn al-Qayyim said: "There is no dispute that he was born, peace be upon him, in the heart of Mecca, and that his birth was in the Year of the Elephant." It is narrated from Ibn Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them both, that he said: (The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, was born in the Year of the Elephant), and this is what no scholar doubts. Khalifa ibn Khayyat, Ibn al-Jazari, Ibn Dihya, and Ibn al-Jawzi have reported consensus on this. As for his birth on Monday, it is narrated from Abu Qatadah al-Ansari, may Allah be pleased with him, that he said: "The Prophet, peace be upon him, was asked about fasting on Mondays. He said: 'That is the day I was born, and the day I was sent (as a prophet) or the revelation was sent down to me.'" Ibn Kathir said: "Far be it, nay, he erred, who said: He was born on Friday the seventeenth of Rabi' al-Awwal." The point of disagreement was in determining the month and the day within it. Ibn Ishaq narrated from a group of the companions of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, that they said to him: "O Messenger of Allah, tell us about yourself." He said: (Yes, I am the supplication of my father Abraham, and the good news of Jesus, and my mother saw when she was pregnant with me that a light emerged from her that illuminated the palaces of Syria for her). His birth, peace be upon him, was after the death of his father Abdullah, as he was a fetus in his mother's womb when his father passed away, so the Prophet, peace be upon him, grew up an orphan. His birth took place in the house of Abu Talib in the valley of Banu Hashim.

575 CE · 49 BH
The first incident of the Prophet's chest being opened.
Honorable Mecca
Anas bin Malik narrated that the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, was playing with some boys when Gabriel, peace be upon him, came to him. Gabriel took him and laid him down, then opened his chest and took out his heart. He extracted a clot from it and said, "This is the devil's share of you." Then he washed it in a golden basin with Zamzam water, then sewed it up, and returned it to its place. The boys came running to his mother – meaning his wet nurse – and said, "Muhammad has been killed!" They met him, and his complexion had changed. Anas said, "I used to see the mark of that stitch on his chest."

575 CE · 48 BH
The death of the Prophet's mother, Aminah bint Wahb, and the upbringing of the Prophet, peace be upon him, by Umm Ayman.
Al-Abwa
When the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, reached six years of age, his mother, the noble Aminah bint Wahb, took him to visit her maternal uncles, Banu Adi ibn al-Najjar, accompanied by his wet nurse, Umm Ayman, may Allah be pleased with her. They were traveling on two camels. His mother disembarked with him at the house of al-Nabighah, near the grave of his father, Abdullah ibn Abd al-Muttalib. She stayed with her relatives for a month, and then his mother returned with him to Mecca. When they reached Al-Abwa, his mother Aminah bint Wahb passed away. Umm Ayman, may Allah be pleased with her – whose name was Barakah bint Tha'labah ibn Hisn – returned with him. Umm Ayman, may Allah be pleased with her, loved the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, with an intense love and cared for him with the utmost attention, protecting him with her care and compassion. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was dutiful to her and treated her kindly. When he grew older, peace and blessings be upon him, he freed her, and then married her to Zayd ibn Harithah, may Allah be pleased with him, by whom she had Usamah ibn Zayd, may Allah be pleased with them both. Umm Ayman, may Allah be pleased with her, passed away five months after the passing of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him.

575 CE · 48 BH
The guardianship of Abdul Muttalib over the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, and his knowledge of his affairs.
The Shadow of the Kaaba
When Aminah, the mother of the Messenger, peace be upon him, passed away while he was six years old, his guardianship fell to his grandfather, Abdul Muttalib ibn Hashim. Abdul Muttalib was a man of honor and virtue among his people, and Quraysh called him "Al-Fadl" (the Virtuous) due to his generosity and merit. He assumed the responsibilities of Rifadah (providing food for pilgrims) and Siqayah (providing water for pilgrims) after the death of his father, Hashim ibn Abd Manaf. Abdul Muttalib loved his grandson Muhammad with a great love, preferring him over all his other sons and showing him more tenderness than he showed his own children. Ibn Ishaq, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, was with his grandfather Abdul Muttalib ibn Hashim – meaning, after the death of his mother Aminah bint Wahb. A bed would be laid for Abdul Muttalib in the shade of the Kaaba, and his sons would sit around that bed until he came out to it. None of his sons would sit on it out of reverence for him. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, would come as a young boy, strong and full, and sit on it. His uncles would try to pull him away from it, but when Abdul Muttalib saw that, he would say, 'Leave my son; by Allah, he has a great destiny.' Then he would seat him with him on his bed, stroke his back with his hand, and be pleased with what he saw him do." Abdul Muttalib would not eat food unless he said, "Bring my son to me!" and he would be brought to him. When Abdul Muttalib was nearing death, he bequeathed to Abu Talib the care and protection of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him.

577 CE · 46 BH
The death of Abdul Muttalib and his bequest of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, to Abu Talib.
Honorable Mecca
When the Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, reached eight years of age, his grandfather, Abd al-Muttalib, passed away. When Abd al-Muttalib was on his deathbed, he entrusted his son, Abu Talib, with the care and protection of the Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, and to look after him. He entrusted him to Abu Talib because Abdullah, the father of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and Abu Talib were from the same mother. So, when Abd al-Muttalib died, Abu Talib was the one who took charge of the affairs of the Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, after his grandfather. Ibn Sa'd and Ibn Asakir narrated from Ibn Abbas, may God be pleased with them, and others, who said: When Abd al-Muttalib passed away, Abu Talib took the Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, and he would stay with him. Abu Talib loved him with an intense love, not loving his own children as much. He would not sleep except next to him, and he would favor him with food. When Abu Talib's children ate together or individually, they would not be full. But if the Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, ate with them, they would be full. When Abu Talib intended to give them breakfast or dinner, he would say, "Wait until my son arrives." Then the Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, would come and eat with them, and they would have leftovers from their food. If he was not with them, their food would not be enough to fill them. If it was milk, he would drink first, then the children would take a large bowl, like a platter, and drink from it until they were all satisfied from that single bowl. One of them would even drink a whole bowl by himself. Abu Talib would then say, "Indeed, you are blessed!"

581 CE · 42 BH
The Prophet's journey with his uncle Abu Talib to Syria.
Busra
When the Prophet, peace be upon him, reached the age of twelve years, his uncle Abu Talib traveled to the Levant on a trade caravan, and he took him along. When the caravan camped at Busra, they passed by a monk there named Bahira, who was knowledgeable in the Gospel and an expert in Christianity. It was there that Bahira saw the Prophet, peace be upon him, and he began to observe him and speak with him. Then he turned to Abu Talib and asked, "Who is this boy to you?" Abu Talib replied, "He is my son." (Abu Talib used to call him his son due to his intense love and compassion for him). Bahira said, "He is not your son, and it is not fitting that the father of this boy should be alive." Abu Talib said, "He is my nephew." Bahira asked, "What happened to his father?" Abu Talib replied, "He died while his mother was pregnant with him." Bahira said, "You have spoken the truth. Return him to his land and beware of the Jews, for by God, if they see him here, they will wish him great harm. For this nephew of yours is destined for a great matter." Abu Talib hastened back to Mecca with him. Al-Tirmidhi narrated that Abu Talib went out to the Levant, and the Prophet, peace be upon him, went out with him along with some elders of Quraysh. When they approached the monk (meaning Bahira), they dismounted and set up their camp. The monk came out to them. Previously, they used to pass by him, but he would not come out to them or pay them any attention. As they were setting up their camp, the monk went among them until he came and took the hand of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, and said, "This is the master of the worlds, this is the Messenger of the Lord of the worlds, whom Allah will send as a mercy to the worlds." Some elders of Quraysh said to him, "What is your knowledge of this?" He replied, "When you approached from the mountain pass, no tree or stone remained but bowed in prostration, and only prophets bow in prostration. And I know him by the Seal of Prophethood below the cartilage of his shoulder, like an apple." Then he went back and prepared food for them. When he brought it to them, and the Prophet, peace be upon him, was tending to the camels, he said, "Send for him." The Prophet, peace be upon him, came, and a cloud shaded him. When he approached the people, he found that they had preceded him to the shade of the tree. When the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, sat down, the shade of the tree moved to cover him. Bahira said, "Look at the shade of the tree, how it has moved to him!" While he was standing among them, he implored them not to take him to the Romans, for if the Romans saw him, they would recognize him by his description and kill him. He looked and saw seven men approaching from the direction of Rome. He met them and asked, "What brings you here?" They said, "We have come because this Prophet is to emerge this month. No road was left without people being sent to it, and we have been informed of his news, so we were sent to this road of yours." He asked, "Did anyone who is better than you precede you?" They said, "We were only informed of his news for this road of yours." He asked, "If Allah wills something to happen, can any person prevent it?" They said, "No." He said, "Then pledge allegiance to him, and they stayed with him." The monk Bahira said, "I adjure you by Allah, who is his guardian?" Abu Talib said, "He kept imploring him until Abu Talib returned him."

585 CE · 39 BH شوال
The War of al-Fijār, and the Prophet's witnessing of it, peace be upon him.
Makkah Al-Mukarramah
When the Messenger of God, peace be upon him, reached fourteen or fifteen years of age, the War of Al-Fijar broke out between Quraysh and those with them from Kinanah, and Qays Aylan. This was one of the greatest days of the Arabs. The cause of this war was that Urwah Al-Rahhal, son of Utbah bin Rabiah, granted protection to a caravan belonging to Al-Nu'man bin Al-Mundhir. Al-Barrad bin Qays, one of the Banu Damrah bin Bakr bin Abd Manat bin Kinanah, said to him, "Will you protect it against Kinanah?" He replied, "Yes, and against all creation." So Urwah Al-Rahhal set out, and Al-Barrad followed him, seeking an opportunity. When they were at Tayman Dhi Dhilal in Al-Aliyah, Urwah was caught unaware. Al-Barrad attacked him and killed him during the sacred month. This is why it was called the War of Al-Fijar. A messenger came to Quraysh and said, "Al-Barrad has killed Urwah during the sacred month at Ukaz." They set out, and Hawazin were unaware. Then the news reached them, and they pursued them, catching up to them before they entered the sanctuary. They fought until nightfall, and then they entered the sanctuary, so Hawazin ceased their pursuit. After this day, the people met for many days, with each tribe of Quraysh and Kinanah having a leader from among them, and each tribe of Qays having a leader from among them. The Messenger of God, peace be upon him, witnessed some of their days, including the Day of Al-Nakhlah, when he was fourteen years old. Some say twenty years old. After their return from it, in Dhu al-Qi'dah, the Pact of Al-Fudul took place. The reason for it was that a man from Zabid, from Yemen, sold goods to Al-As bin Wa'il Al-Sahmi, who delayed paying him the price. The man climbed Mount Abu Qubais and cried out, mentioning his grievance. Quraysh then formed the Pact of Al-Fudul to support the oppressed. The Prophet, peace be upon him, witnessed this pact with them.

591 CE · 33 BH ذي
The Pact of the Virtuous among the tribes of Quraysh.
The house of Abdullah ibn Jud'an
The Hilf al-Fudul (Alliance of the Virtuous) took place in a sacred month. Tribes of Quraysh were called to it: Banu Hashim, Banu Muttalib, Asad ibn Abd al-Uzza, Zuhrah ibn Kilab, and Taym ibn Murrah. They gathered in the house of Abdullah ibn Jud'an al-Taymi, due to his age and nobility. They made a pact and a covenant that if they found anyone oppressed in Mecca, whether from its people or others, they would stand with him and be against whoever oppressed him until his rights were restored. The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, witnessed this alliance and said about it: "I witnessed an alliance in the house of Abdullah ibn Jud'an that I would not exchange for a herd of red camels, and if I were called to it in Islam, I would respond." This alliance contradicted the spirit of tribalistic pride that partisanship stirred. It is said that the reason for this alliance was that a man from Zubayd came to Mecca with merchandise, which Al-As ibn Wail al-Sahmi bought from him but withheld his payment. The man sought help from the allied tribes of Abd al-Dar, Makhzum, Jumah, Sahm, and Adi, but they paid him no heed. He then climbed Mount Abu Qubays and called out with verses describing his oppression, raising his voice. Al-Zubayr ibn Abd al-Muttalib then went forth and said, "This cannot be left as is." They gathered and formed the alliance known as Hilf al-Fudul. Then they went to Al-As ibn Wail and retrieved the Zubaydi's right. The reason for its naming is that three men from the Jurhum tribe, namely Al-Fadl ibn Fadalah, Al-Fadl ibn Wada'ah, and Al-Fadl ibn al-Harith, had previously made a similar agreement. When the actions of the Quraysh resembled the actions of these early Jurhumis, all of whom were named Al-Fadl, the alliance was named Hilf al-Fudul.

595 CE · 28 BH
The marriage of the Prophet, peace be upon him, to Khadijah bint Khuwaylid, may Allah be pleased with her.
Makkah Al-Mukarramah
There are differing opinions regarding the age of the Prophet, peace be upon him, and the age of Khadijah when they married. It has been said that he was twenty-five. It has also been said that he was twenty-seven. And it has been said that he was thirty. And other opinions have been stated. As for the age of Khadijah, the sayings also vary between thirty-five, forty, and other ages. When Khadijah, may Allah be pleased with her, heard about the Prophet, peace be upon him, his trustworthiness, and his noble character, it is narrated that Khadijah's sister hired the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, and his partner for a journey. When they completed the journey, some payment was still due to her. Her partner began to approach them to collect the dues, and he would say to Muhammad, peace be upon him: "Go." Muhammad would reply: "You go, for I feel shy." One time, when her partner came to them, she asked: "Where is Muhammad? Why doesn't he come with you?" He said: "I told him, but he claimed he felt shy." She mentioned this to her sister Khadijah, who said: "I have never seen a man more shy, more chaste, or... than Muhammad, peace be upon him." This impressed Khadijah, so she sent for him and said: "Go to my father and propose to him." He said: "Your father is a man of great wealth, and he will not agree to this." She said: "Go, meet him and speak to him, and I will handle the rest." The Prophet, peace be upon him, married her while she was a widow.

596 CE · 28 BH ذي
The Prophet's journey to Syria for the second time.
Bosra
When the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, reached twenty-five years of age, he had no name in Mecca except "Al-Amin" (The Trustworthy), due to the perfection of his good qualities. His uncle, Abu Talib, said to him: "O nephew, I am a man with no wealth, and times have become harsh upon us, and we have been afflicted by severe years. We have no provisions or trade. Your people's caravan is ready to depart for Syria, and Khadijah bint Khuwaylid is sending men from your people with her caravans to trade her wealth, and they gain profits. If you were to approach her and offer yourself for her business, she would hasten to you and prefer you over others, due to what she has heard of your purity. Although I dislike you going to Syria and fear for you from the Jews, we have no choice." Khadijah bint Khuwaylid was a merchant woman of noble lineage, great wealth, and extensive trade that she sent to Syria. Her caravans were like those of the rest of Quraysh. She used to hire men and give them money for trade on a profit-sharing basis. News of the Prophet's truthful speech, his great trustworthiness, and his noble character reached Khadijah. So, she sent for him to manage her trade. He set out with her servant Maysarah until they reached Syria. His uncles had instructed the caravan members to look after him until he reached Syria. They camped in the market of Busra, under a tree near the monastery of a monk named Nestorius. The monk looked at Maysarah, whom he recognized, and said: "O Maysarah, who is this who has camped under this tree?" Maysarah replied: "A man from Quraysh, from the people of the Sanctuary." The monk said: "No one has ever camped under this tree except a prophet." Then the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, sold his goods, meaning his merchandise, and bought what he intended to buy. He then set off, returning to Mecca with Maysarah. When he arrived in Mecca with Khadijah's wealth, she sold what had come back for double its value or close to it. Maysarah told her about the monk's words and what he had witnessed of the Prophet's affairs. Thereupon, she proposed herself to him, may Allah be pleased with her. She was of the noblest lineage among the women of Quraysh, the most honorable, and the wealthiest. When she said this to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, he mentioned it to his uncles. His uncle Hamzah went with him and approached Khuwaylid ibn Asad to propose marriage. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, married her. She was the first woman he married, and he did not marry anyone else until she passed away, may Allah be pleased with her.

610 CE · 13 BH رمضان
The first revelation and the prophethood of the Prophet, peace be upon him.
Cave of Hira
The first form of revelation the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) received was true visions in his sleep. He would not see a vision except it would come to pass like the breaking of the dawn. Then, he began to love solitude, so he would retreat to the cave of Hira and worship there. While he was in the cave, Gabriel (peace be upon him) came to him with the divine words: "{Read, [O Muhammad], by which your Lord created (1) Created man from a clinging substance. (2) Read, and your Lord is the most generous. (3) [He] who taught by the pen. (4) Taught man that which he knew not. (5)}" [Al-Alaq: 1-5]. He then recounted this to his wife Khadijah, who in turn told Warqah ibn Nawfal. Warqah confirmed it, saying, "This is the greatest law (Namus) that came to Moses." Then, the revelation ceased for a period. After that, Gabriel returned to him while he was sitting on a chair. The Prophet (peace be upon him) was frightened by him and returned to his family, saying, "Wrap me, wrap me." Then Allah the Almighty revealed: "{O you who enshrouds himself! (1) Arise and warn. (2) And your Lord magnify. (3) And your clothing purify. (4) And uncleanliness abandon. (5)}" [Al-Muddaththir: 1-5]. This marked the beginning of his prophethood and the command to convey the message.

Undetermined

613 CE · 9 BH
What the Companions suffered from the persecution of Quraysh.
The Valley of Mecca
The first seven to openly declare their Islam were: the Messenger of God, peace be upon him, Abu Bakr, Ammar, his mother Sumayyah, Suhayb, Bilal, and Al-Miqdad. As for the Messenger of God, peace be upon him, God protected him through his uncle Abu Talib. As for Abu Bakr, his people protected him. As for the rest of them, the polytheists seized them, clothed them in iron armor, and roasted them in the sun. None of them escaped unharmed except that they complied with what the polytheists wanted and deceived them, except for Bilal. His soul was dear to him for the sake of God, the Almighty, and he was insignificant to his people. They seized him, gave him to the children, who paraded him through the valleys of Mecca, while he said: "One, one." They put a rope around his neck and handed him over to the children to play with, until it left a mark on his neck. Bilal belonged to some of the Banu Jumah. The one who took the lead in torturing him was Umayyah ibn Khalaf. He would take him out when the midday heat was intense, throw him on his back in the open ground of Mecca, then order a large rock to be placed on his chest. Then he would say to him: "You will remain like this until you die, or disbelieve in Muhammad and worship Al-Lat and Al-Uzza." While enduring this torture and affliction, he would say: "One, one." It was as if his torture and affliction only increased his faith upon faith. Abu Bakr felt compassion for him when he saw him one day in this extreme humiliation, so he bought him and set him free, and he also freed six others who were being tortured for Islam.

613 CE · 9 BH
The Quraysh asked Abu Talib to restrain the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, from them.
Makkah al-Mukarramah
The Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, called to God Almighty in all his states; by night and by day, in secret and in public. Nothing diverted him from this, no one turned him back, and no obstacle prevented him. He followed people to their gatherings, assemblies, and congregations, and during festivals and the pilgrimage seasons, calling to whoever he met, whether free or slave, weak or strong, rich or poor. The disbelievers of his people, the polytheists of Quraysh, oppressed him and those who followed him. Among the harshest of them towards him were his uncle Abu Lahab and his wife Umm Jamil, the firewood carrier – Arwa bint Harb bin Umayya, the sister of Abu Sufyan. His uncle Abu Talib bin Abdul Muttalib, however, was compassionate towards him, treated him well, defended him, and protected him, opposing his people in this matter, even though he remained on their religion. His continued adherence to his people's religion was part of God Almighty's wisdom and a protection He provided for His Messenger. For had Abu Talib embraced Islam, he would have had no standing or influence among the polytheists of Quraysh, nor would they have feared or respected him. The biographers narrated that Quraysh came to Abu Talib and said, "Your nephew has annoyed us in our gathering place and our mosque; stop him from us." He said, "O Aqil, go and bring Muhammad to me." So he brought him in the heat of the afternoon. When he came to them, Abu Talib said, "These cousins of yours claim that you annoy them in their gathering place and their mosque; so cease annoying them." The Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, raised his gaze to the sky and said, "Do you see this sun?" They said, "Yes." After the defeat of the polytheists at Badr, Abu Sufyan vowed that no water from ritual impurity would touch his head until he invaded Muhammad, peace be upon him, and his companions and took revenge on the Muslims. So he set out with two hundred riders from Quraysh, to fulfill his oath. He reached the outskirts of Medina at night and sought refuge with Banu Nadir. He went to Huyayy ibn Akhtab and knocked on his door, but Huyayy refused to open it for him, fearing him. So Abu Sufyan turned away from him to Salam ibn Mishkam, who was the chief of Banu Nadir at that time and the keeper of their treasure. Abu Sufyan asked for permission to enter, and he was granted it. Salam hosted him, gave him wine to drink, and informed him of the news of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him. Then Abu Sufyan left in the latter part of that night and went to his companions. He sent some men from Quraysh, who went to an area of Medina called Al-Arid – a valley in Medina. They set fire to the fruit-bearing trees and date palms of the Muslims. They found a man from the Ansar and his ally in their fields and killed them, then they turned back. When this news reached the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, he set out in pursuit of them with two hundred of the Emigrants and Helpers. He appointed Abu Lubaba Bashir ibn Abd al-Mundhir, may Allah be pleased with him, as governor of Medina. Abu Sufyan and his companions began to throw down sacks of saweeq – roasted wheat or barley, then ground, and used as provisions, mixed with water, ghee, or honey – which was the majority of their provisions, to lighten their load for escape. The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, reached Qaraqarah al-Kadr – Qaraqarah: level ground. Al-Kadr: a water source belonging to Banu Sulaym, and the origin of Kadr is a bird with a dusky color, after which the place or water was named. Then he turned back towards Medina, as Abu Sufyan and his companions had escaped him. The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, was away for five days. When the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, returned with them, the Muslims said, "O Messenger of Allah, do you hope that this will be a raid for us?" He said, "Yes." This raid is also called the "Raid of Saweeq" because the most of what the people threw away from their provisions was saweeq, so the Muslims returned with a lot of saweeq. This occurred in Shawwal in the second year of the Hijra.

624 CE · 3 AH
The Killing of Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf.
Al-Madinah Al-Munawwarah
Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf was a man from the tribe of Tayy, and his mother was from the Banu Nadir, a Jewish tribe. He was a poet who used to lampoon the Prophet, peace be upon him, and his companions, inciting hatred against them and causing them harm. When the Battle of Badr occurred, he was disheartened and humiliated, saying, "The belly of the earth is better than its surface today." He then went to Mecca, wept for the slain Quraysh, and incited them with poetry. Jabir ibn Abdullah narrated that the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, said, "Who will deal with Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf? For he has indeed harmed Allah and His Messenger." Muhammad ibn Maslamah said, "O Messenger of Allah, would you like me to kill him?" He replied, "Yes." He said, "Grant me permission to speak." The Prophet said, "Speak." So, Muhammad ibn Maslamah went to Ka'b and said to him, mentioning what transpired between them, "This man has asked for charity and has burdened us." When Ka'b heard this, he said, "And by Allah, you will soon be weary of him." Muhammad replied, "We have followed him now, and we dislike leaving him until we see what becomes of his affair. He also said, "And I wanted you to lend me some money." Ka'b asked, "What will you give me as collateral?" Muhammad replied, "What do you want?" Ka'b said, "You will give us your women as collateral." Muhammad replied, "You are the most handsome of the Arabs; shall we give you our women as collateral!?" Ka'b then said, "You will give me your children as collateral." Muhammad replied, "One of our children is insulted, and it is said, 'He was ransomed for two measures of dates.' But we will give you our weapons as collateral." Ka'b said, "Then fine." He promised to bring him Al-Harith, Abu 'Abs ibn Jabr, and 'Abbad ibn Bishr. Muhammad said, "So they came and called him at night, and he came down to them." Sufyan said, "Others than 'Amr said: His wife said to him, 'I hear a sound like the sound of blood.' He said, 'It is only Muhammad ibn Maslamah, his companion, and Abu Na'ilah. Indeed, the generous man, if invited to a stab in the night, would respond.' Muhammad said, 'When he comes, I will reach for his head, and when I have a firm grip on him, then it is yours.' He said, 'When he came down, he was wearing perfume. They said, "We detect the scent of perfume on you." He said, "Yes, so-and-so is under me; she is the most fragrant of the Arab women." He said, "Will you permit me to smell it?" He said, "Yes, smell it." So he took it and smelled it, then said, "Will you permit me to return?" He said, "He gained a firm grip on his head, then said, 'It is yours.'" He said, "So they killed him." (...Then they came to the Prophet, peace be upon him, and informed him.)

624 CE · 3 AH صفر
The raid of Ghatafan on Najd (the Raid of Dhu Amr).
Dhu Ammar
This was the Ghazwah of Dhu Amarr, in the region of Najd. The reason for it was that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, received news that a group from Banu Tha'labah ibn Sa'id ibn Dhubyan ibn Baghidh ibn Ra'ith ibn Ghatafan, and Banu Muharib ibn Khasafah ibn Qays, at Dhu Amarr, had gathered, intending to raid the frontiers of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him. A man from them, called Du'thur ibn Al-Harith ibn Muharib, had united them. So, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, summoned the Muslims and set out with four hundred and fifty men, accompanied by several horses. He appointed Uthman ibn Affan as his deputy in Medina. They encountered a man from them at Dhu al-Qassah, called Jabbar from Banu Tha'labah. The Muslims asked him, "Where are you going?" He replied, "I am going to Yathrib to scout for myself and look around." He was brought to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and informed him of their news. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said, "They will not meet you. If they hear of your march, they will flee to the mountain tops. I am going with you." So, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, invited Jabbar to Islam, and he embraced Islam. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, attached him to Bilal, and Jabbar led them along a path, bringing them down to them. The people heard of the march of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and fled to the mountain tops. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, reached a water source called Dhu Amarr and camped there. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and his companions were struck by heavy rain, and the clothes of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and his companions became wet. So, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, descended under a tree and spread his clothes to dry, and lay down, while the polytheists could see him. The Muslims were occupied with their affairs. The polytheists sent a brave man from them, called Du'thur ibn Al-Harith, who was their chief and bravest. He was girded with a sword. Du'thur rushed forward, drawing his sword, until he stood over the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, with his sword drawn, and said, "O Muhammad, who will protect you from me today?" The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, replied, "Allah." Gabriel pushed him in the chest, and the sword fell from his hand. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, took it and said to him, "Who will protect you from me?!" He replied, "No one." And I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah. By Allah, I will never gather a force against you again. So, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, gave him his sword. Then he came to his people, and they asked him, "What happened to you? Woe to you!" He said, "I saw a tall man who pushed me in the chest, and I fell on my back. I knew he was an angel, and I testified that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah. By Allah, I will never gather a force against him again." And he began to call his people to Islam. And Allah, the Almighty, revealed: "O you who have believed, remember the favor of Allah upon you when a people intended to extend their hands [in aggression] against you, but He restrained their hands from you." [Al-Ma'idah, 11]. And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, returned to Medina without encountering any harm. His absence was fifteen nights. It was also said that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, stayed in Najd for the entire month of Safar.

624 CE · 3 AH ربيع
The Battle of Bu'ran between Al-Fur' and Medina.
Two seas
The Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, went on an expedition intending to confront Quraysh, and he appointed Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum, may God be pleased with him, as his deputy in Medina. He proceeded until he reached Buhran, in the vicinity of al-Fur'. The reason for this expedition was that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, learned that a large gathering of Banu Sulaym ibn Mansur was present there. So he set out with three hundred of his companions, and he did not reveal the purpose of his journey. When he was one night's journey from Buhran, he encountered a man from Banu Sulaym, who informed them that the people had dispersed. He detained the man with another companion and proceeded until he arrived at Buhran, finding no one there. He stayed for a few days, then returned without encountering any hostility, and released the man. Then he turned back, returning to Medina.

624 CE · 3 AH جمادى
The expedition of Zayd ibn Harithah.
Monkeys
After their defeat in the Battle of Badr, the polytheists of Mecca began searching for another route for their trade to Syria. Some of them suggested the route of Najd in Iraq, and they indeed took it. Merchants set out from them, including Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, Safwan ibn Umayyah, and Huwaytib ibn Abd al-Uzza, carrying silver and many goods valued at one hundred thousand dirhams. This news reached the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, through Sulait ibn al-Nu'man, may Allah be pleased with him. So, he sent Zaid ibn Harithah with one hundred riders to intercept the caravan. Zaid encountered the caravan at a watering place called al-Qardah, which is one of the watering places of Najd. Its men fled in terror, and the Muslims seized the caravan and its contents. They captured its guide, Furat ibn Hayyan, who embraced Islam at the hands of the Prophet, peace be upon him. They returned to Medina, and the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, distributed one-fifth of the spoils and divided the remainder among the members of the expedition.

625 CE · 3 AH شوال
The Battle of Uhud and the Reason for the Muslims' Defeat.
Uhud
The reason for this was that when the polytheists suffered casualties among their nobles at the Battle of Badr, and the caravan with its trade goods, which was with Abu Sufyan, escaped, upon their return to Mecca, the sons of those who were killed and the leaders of those who remained said to Abu Sufyan: "Amass these funds for the war against Muhammad." So they spent them for that purpose, gathered armies and allied tribes, and marched with nearly three thousand men, until they encamped near Uhud, facing Medina. On Friday, the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, prayed. After finishing, he prayed over a man from Banu Najjar, named Malik ibn Amr. He then consulted the people, asking whether they should march out to meet the enemy or remain in Medina. Abdullah ibn Ubayy suggested staying in Medina. He argued that if they stayed, they would be in a terrible confinement, and if the enemy entered, the men would fight them face-to-face, while the women and children would pelt them with stones from above. If the enemy retreated, they would return in defeat. Other Companions, who had not witnessed Badr, suggested marching out to meet them. The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, went inside, donned his armor, and marched out to meet them. He set out with a thousand of his Companions. When they reached a place called Ash-Shawtt, Abdullah ibn Ubayy turned back with a third of the army, angered because his opinion had not been heeded. He and his followers said: "If we knew there would be fighting today, we would have followed you, but we do not see you fighting today." The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, continued his march until he reached the valley of Uhud, at the entrance of the valley. He positioned his back and his camp towards Uhud and prepared for battle with seven hundred of his Companions. He appointed Abdullah ibn Jubayr, brother of Banu Amr ibn Awf, as the commander of the archers, who numbered fifty men that day. He instructed them: "Repel the cavalry from us with arrows, so they do not come at us from behind. Whether we gain victory or suffer defeat, remain in your position, so that we are not attacked from your side." The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, wore two coats of mail and gave the banner to Mus'ab ibn Umayr, brother of Banu Abd ad-Dar. The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, permitted some of the young boys to participate that day and deferred others. Quraysh mobilized, numbering three thousand men, with two hundred horses prepared for battle. They appointed Khalid ibn al-Walid to command the right flank of the cavalry and Ikrimah ibn Abi Jahl to command the left flank. The banner was given to Banu Abd ad-Dar. Then, what happened between the two factions occurred. Initial signs of victory began to appear for the Muslims. When the Muslims saw the polytheists retreating, the archers neglected the Prophet's command to them and descended to collect spoils. Khalid ibn al-Walid seized the opportunity, circled behind them, and engaged them in battle, which led to a reversal of fortunes. The battle concluded with the martyrdom of seventy Muslims, including Hamzah ibn Abd al-Muttalib, Mus'ab ibn Umayr, Abdullah ibn Haram, the father of Jabir, and Abdullah ibn Jubayr, the commander of the archers.

Uhud

625 CE · 3 AH شوال
The Battle of Hamra al-Asad.
Hamra' al-Asad
Ibn Kathir said in his Tafsir of the verse: "{Those who responded to Allah and the Messenger, even after they had been wounded}" This was on the day of Hamra' al-Asad. When the polytheists inflicted what they inflicted upon the Muslims – meaning on the day of Uhud – they turned back towards their lands. As they continued on their journey, they regretted not having finished off the people of Medina and made it the decisive blow. When this reached the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, he called upon the Muslims to go after them to instill fear in them and show them that they possessed strength and resilience. He did not permit anyone except those who had been present at the battle of Uhud, except for Jabir ibn Abdullah, may Allah be pleased with him. The Muslims responded, despite their wounds and exhaustion, in obedience to Allah the Almighty and His Messenger, peace be upon him. It was narrated from Aisha, may Allah be pleased with her, that she said regarding the verse: "{Those who responded to Allah and the Messenger, even after they had been wounded – for those among them who did good and feared Allah will have a great reward.}" She said to Urwah: "O son of my sister, your fathers were among them: Al-Zubayr and Abu Bakr. When what happened to the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, happened on the day of Uhud, and the polytheists departed from him, he feared they might return. He said: 'Who will go after them?' So seventy men volunteered from them. She said: 'Abu Bakr and Al-Zubayr were among them.'" Ibn Kathir said after mentioning this Hadith: "(This narration is very strange, for it is well-known among the scholars of Maghazi that those who went out with the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, to Hamra' al-Asad were all who had witnessed Uhud, and they were seven hundred. Seventy of them were killed, and the rest remained)." The apparent meaning is that there is no contradiction between the words of Aisha and the scholars of Maghazi, because the meaning of her statement: "So seventy men volunteered for it" is that they preceded others, and then the rest joined them. Hamra' al-Asad is located eight miles from Medina.

625 CE · 4 AH محرم
The expedition of Abdullah ibn Abi Sarh, may Allah be pleased with him, to Qatan.
Dhi Qatn
The expedition of Abu Salamah Abdullah bin Abdul-Asad, who was the cousin of the Prophet, peace be upon him, through his paternal aunt Barrah bint Abdul-Muttalib, and his foster brother, as they were both nursed by Thuwibah. Qatan is a mountain, and it is also said to be a water source belonging to Banu Asad. The reason for this expedition was that a man from Tayy, named Al-Walid bin Zuhayr bin Tarif, came to Medina to visit his niece, Zaynab, who was married to Tulayb bin Umayr bin Wahb. He was informed that Tulayhah and Salamah, the sons of Khuwaylid, had gathered their people and those who obeyed them to wage war against the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him. Qays bin Al-Harith advised them against it, but they disobeyed. When this reached the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, he summoned Abu Salamah, may Allah be pleased with him, and said, "Go forth on this expedition, for I have appointed you in command of it." He tied a banner for him and said, "Travel until you reach the land of Banu Asad bin Khuzaymah, and raid them before their forces can gather against you." He also advised him to fear Allah, the Almighty, and to treat the Muslims with him well. So, one hundred and fifty men went with him on this expedition, and the man from Tayy accompanied them as a guide. He traveled swiftly, diverting them from the main road, and journeyed day and night. They outpaced any news and arrived at Dhi Qatan, a water source of Banu Asad, where their forces were gathered. They raided their livestock and captured three shepherds, while the rest escaped. They went to their people and informed them of the news, warning them about Abu Salamah's forces and exaggerating their numbers. Consequently, the gathered forces dispersed in all directions. Abu Salamah arrived at the water source to find the gathering had dispersed. He made camp and divided his companions into three groups to pursue the camels and sheep. One group remained with him, while the other two raided in different directions. He instructed them not to go too far in their pursuit and to return to him if they were safe, and not to separate. He appointed a leader from among them for each group. They all returned to him safely, having acquired camels and sheep, and without encountering anyone. Abu Salamah then headed back to Medina with all the spoils, accompanied by the man from Tayy. After they had traveled for a night, Abu Salamah divided the spoils, setting aside the special share for the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, which was a slave. He also set aside the fifth (khums) and gave the guide from Tayy his satisfaction from the spoils. Then he divided the remainder among his companions, and each person received seven camels. He arrived in Medina with all of this without facing any opposition.

625 CE · 4 AH محرم
The expedition of Abdullah ibn Unays al-Juhani to Sufyan ibn Khalid in Batn Urnah.
The valley of Urnah
Abdullah bin Unais, may Allah be pleased with him, departed from Medina on Monday, five nights into Muharram, thirty-five months after the Hijra of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him. It reached the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, that Sufyan bin Khalid bin Nabih al-Hudhali, then al-Lahyani – who resided in Urnah and its vicinity with some of his people and others – intended to gather forces against the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, and that many people from various backgrounds had joined him. Abdullah bin Unais, may Allah Almighty be pleased with him, said: The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, summoned me and said: "It has reached me that Sufyan bin Khalid bin Nabih is gathering people against me to attack me, and he is in Nakhla or Urnah. Go to him and kill him." I said: O Messenger of Allah, describe him to me so I can recognize him. He said: "The sign between you and him is that when you see him, you will be awestruck and fearful of him, and you will feel a shiver, and you will remember Satan." Abdullah said: I was not afraid of men, so I said: O Messenger of Allah, I have never been afraid of anything. He said: "Yes; that is the sign between you and him: you will feel a shiver when you see him." He said: I asked permission from the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, to speak. He said: "Say what you wish." And he said: "Claim kinship with Khuzā'ah." So I took my sword and added nothing to it, and I went out claiming kinship with Khuzā'ah. When I reached the valley of Urnah, I met him walking, with a group of Abyssinians behind him. When I saw him, I was awestruck by him and recognized him by the description the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, had given me. I said: Allah and His Messenger have spoken the truth. The time for Asr prayer had arrived when I saw him, so I prayed while walking, gesturing with my head. When I approached him, he said: "Who is the man?" I said: "A man from Khuzā'ah. I heard about your gathering against Muhammad, so I came to be with you in it." He said: "Yes; I am indeed among those gathered against him." So I walked with him and talked to him. He found my conversation pleasant, and I recited poetry to him, saying: "How strange is what Muhammad has introduced of this new religion! He has abandoned the ways of our fathers and deemed their wisdom foolish." He said: "I have not met anyone like me, nor anyone who fights as well as I do." He was leaning on a staff, striking the ground with it, until he reached his tent, and his companions dispersed to nearby dwellings, while they surrounded him. He said: "Come here, O brother of Khuzā'ah." I approached him. He said: "Sit down." I sat with him until the people calmed down and slept. I took advantage of the opportunity. In most narrations, it is said that I walked with him until, when I had the opportunity, I struck him with my sword and killed him, and took his head. Then I turned and ascended a mountain, and entered a cave. The pursuit of horses and men came from all directions, while I was hidden in the cave. A man approached with his water skin and sandals in his hand. I was afraid. He put down his water skin and sandals and sat down to urinate near the mouth of the cave. I traveled by night and hid by day until I came to Medina. I found the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, in the mosque. When he saw me, he said: "May your face be successful." I said: "And may your face be successful, O Messenger of Allah." I placed the head before him and told him my story. He gave me a staff and said: "Use this to lean on in Paradise; for those who lean on staffs in Paradise are few." The staff remained with Abdullah bin Unais until his death approached. He instructed his family to wrap the staff in his shroud. They did so. The Calm

625 CE · 4 AH صفر
The Expedition of Bi'r Ma'unah.
Bi'r Ma'unah
Anas bin Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated that Ri'l, Dhakwan, 'Usayyah, and Bani Lihyan sought the help of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, against an enemy. 629 CE · 8 AH جمادى
The Battle of Mu'tah.
Mu'tah
The Messenger of God, peace be upon him, dispatched a detachment of three thousand fighters, with Zayd ibn Harithah as their commander. If he were killed, then Ja'far ibn Abi Talib would take command, and if he were killed, then Abdullah ibn Rawahah would take command. The Messenger of God, peace be upon him, saw them off and bid them farewell, then he returned. The army set out. When they reached Ma'an in the land of Sham, the news reached them that Heraclius, the king of Rome, had descended upon the land of Bani Ma'ab – the land of Balqa – with one hundred thousand Romans and another hundred thousand from the Christians of Sham. The Muslims stayed in Ma'an for two nights, consulting about engaging their enemy, who numbered two hundred thousand. They said, "Let us write to the Messenger of God, peace be upon him, to inform him of the number of our enemy, so that he may command us or send us reinforcements." Abdullah ibn Rawahah said, "O people, what you dislike is what you set out to seek – meaning martyrdom. We do not fight people with numbers or strength; rather, we fight them with this [faith] with which God has honored us. So, march forth, for it is one of two good outcomes: either victory or martyrdom." The army agreed with this opinion and set out. When they reached the borders of Balqa, they encountered some of the legions with Heraclius near a village called Mu'tah. They fought, and Zayd ibn Harithah was killed. Ja'far ibn Abi Talib took the banner, and fought until his right hand was severed. He took the banner with his left hand, and it was severed. He then embraced it and was killed in that manner. Abdullah ibn Rawahah took the banner and hesitated for a moment before dismounting, then he resolved and fought until he was killed. Thabit ibn Aqram took the banner and said, "O Muslims, agree upon one of your own." They said, "You." He said, "No." The people agreed upon Khalid ibn al-Walid. When he took the banner, he defended the people and rallied them. Then he retreated, and they retreated with him, until he withdrew with the Muslims who were with him. Khalid ibn al-Walid said, "On the day of Mu'tah, nine swords broke in my hand, and nothing remained in my hand but a Yemeni blade." This implies that they inflicted heavy casualties upon the enemy, for otherwise, they would not have been able to extricate themselves. For this reason and others, some scholars have concluded that the Muslims were victorious in this battle and were not defeated. Anas, may God be pleased with him, reported that the Prophet, peace be upon him, informed the people of the deaths of Zayd, Ja'far, and Ibn Rawahah before their news reached them. He said, "Zayd took the banner and was struck down. Then Ja'far took it and was struck down. Then Ibn Rawahah took it and was struck down, and his eyes were shedding tears, until a sword from the swords of God took it, and God granted them victory."

630 CE · 8 AH ذي
The Battle of Hunayn (Hawazin).
Longing
Hunayn: A valley next to Dhi al-Majaz, near Ta'if. It is a dozen miles from Mecca, in the direction of al-Sharai' and the Great Flood. It is said that it was named Hunayn after a man called Hunayn ibn Qabithah ibn Mahla'il. The Prophet, peace be upon him, had stayed in Mecca for 19 days until Hawazin and Thaqif came and camped in Hunayn, intending to fight the Prophet, peace be upon him. They had gathered before that when they heard of the departure of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, from Medina, thinking that he was coming for them. When they learned that he had arrived in Mecca, they began to prepare to confront him. They intended it to be a decisive battle, so they mobilized their wealth, women, and children so that none of them would flee and abandon their families and property. Their leader was Malik ibn 'Awf al-Nadri, and they mobilized Ghatafan and others with them. The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, prepared to confront them. He borrowed thirty camels and thirty shields from Ya'la ibn Umayyah, and borrowed one hundred shields from Safwan ibn Umayyah. He appointed 'Attab ibn Asid ibn Abi al-'As as governor of Mecca. It is confirmed in the two Sahih collections that the freed slaves (al-tulaqa') went out with him to Hunayn. The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, met the enemy with his army in the valley of Hunayn in the early morning. They descended into it, and upon entering the valley, they attacked Hawazin, who were routed. The Muslims then fell upon the spoils they had left behind. While they were in this state, Hawazin confronted them and rained down a volley of arrows upon them. The Muslims had not expected this, and the earth became straitened for them. They turned and fled, not looking back. The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, turned to the right, saying: "Where are the people? Come to me! I am the Messenger of Allah! I am the Messenger of Allah! I am Muhammad ibn Abdullah." The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, ordered his uncle Al-Abbas – who had a loud voice – to call out to the people to stand firm, specifically mentioning the companions of the Pledge of Ridwan. They rushed to him, then he called out to the Ansar, then to the Banu al-Harith ibn al-Khazraj, and they flew to him, saying: "Labbayk, Labbayk" (Here we are, here we are). A fierce battle ensued against Hawazin. When the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, saw the battle intensifying, he said: "This is when the furnace has heated up." Then he took some pebbles – or dust – and threw them at the faces of the disbelievers, saying: "May the faces be disfigured." No one among them whom Allah created was spared from having his eyes filled with dust from that handful. They turned and fled, and the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, said: "By the Lord of Muhammad, they are defeated." In another narration: "By the Lord of the Ka'bah, they are defeated! By the Lord of the Ka'bah, they are defeated!" It has been narrated that the killed from Banu Malik of Thaqif alone reached 70. At Awstas, 300 from Banu Malik were killed, and a large number from Banu Nasr ibn Mu'awiyah, then from Banu Ri'ab, were killed. It has been narrated that the captives of Hunayn, women and children, numbered 6,000. Meanwhile, four Muslims were killed.

The Battle of Tabuk and the Army of Hardship.
Tabuk
Tabuk is a place between Wadi al-Qura and Sham. Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, said: "My companions sent me to the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, to ask him for mounts for them, as they were with him in the Army of Hardship, which was the expedition to Tabuk..." The hadith of Abu Musa, may Allah be pleased with him, indicates the severe hardship the Companions faced in terms of wealth, provisions, and mounts. As narrated by Muslim, also from Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, the Muslims suffered from a lack of provisions on the way to this expedition, to the extent that they sucked the date pits and drank water with them. In another narration by Muslim, they asked the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, for permission to slaughter their mounts to eat. And Allah Almighty said: {Allah has turned in forgiveness to the Prophet and to the Emigrants and Helpers who followed him in the hour of hardship, after the hearts of a party of them had falved. Ibn Kathir said: The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, resolved to fight the Romans because they were the closest people to him and the most deserving of being invited to the truth due to their proximity to Islam and its people. The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, clarified the objective of this expedition. From Mu'adh, may Allah be pleased with him, it is narrated that the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, said: "You will arrive tomorrow, Allah willing, at the spring of Tabuk.
